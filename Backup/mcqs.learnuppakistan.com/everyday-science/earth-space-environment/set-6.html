Question 1/20 🔥 0 streak

Which instrument is used to record the vibrations produced by an earthquake?

💡 Explanation

A seismograph is an instrument that measures and records details of earthquakes, such as force and duration.

The point within the Earth where an earthquake rupture starts is called the:

💡 Explanation

The focus, or hypocenter, is the point inside the Earth where the earthquake begins, while the epicenter is the point on the surface directly above it.

Which scale is used to measure the magnitude of an earthquake based on the energy released?

💡 Explanation

The Richter scale measures the seismic energy released by an earthquake, whereas the Mercalli scale measures intensity based on observed damage.

Tsunamis are primarily generated by:

💡 Explanation

Tsunamis are large sea waves caused by the displacement of water, most commonly triggered by undersea earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, or landslides.

What type of volcano is characterized by broad, gently sloping sides formed by the eruption of low-viscosity lava?

💡 Explanation

Shield volcanoes have a broad, low profile and are built by the accumulation of fluid basaltic lava flows.

The 'Ring of Fire' is a major area in the basin of which ocean where many earthquakes and volcanic eruptions occur?

💡 Explanation

The Ring of Fire is a path along the Pacific Ocean characterized by active volcanoes and frequent seismic activity due to plate tectonics.

Which seismic waves arrive first at a seismograph station?

💡 Explanation

Primary waves (P-waves) are longitudinal waves and are the fastest seismic waves, meaning they reach recording stations before S-waves.

What is the term for a volcanic vent through which volcanic gases and steam escape?

💡 Explanation

A fumarole is an opening in or near a volcano, through which hot sulfurous gases and steam are emitted.

The Mercalli scale measures which of the following characteristics of an earthquake?

💡 Explanation

The Modified Mercalli Intensity Scale measures the intensity of an earthquake based on the observed effects and damage on the Earth's surface.

Which of the following is considered the most destructive type of seismic wave?

💡 Explanation

Surface waves (Love and Rayleigh waves) travel along the Earth's surface and cause the most structural damage during an earthquake.

The boundary where two tectonic plates move toward each other, leading to subduction or mountain building, is called:

💡 Explanation

Convergent boundaries occur where plates move toward each other, often resulting in deep-sea trenches, volcanic arcs, and mountain ranges.

What is the molten rock called once it has erupted onto the Earth's surface?

💡 Explanation

Magma is molten rock located beneath the surface, while lava is the term used for molten rock that has reached the Earth's surface.

Which phenomenon describes the sudden loss of soil strength, causing saturated soil to behave like a liquid during an earthquake?

💡 Explanation

Soil liquefaction happens when intense shaking causes water-saturated granular soil to lose its mechanical strength and behave like a fluid.

What type of volcano is formed by alternate layers of lava flows and volcanic ash, typically tall and cone-shaped?

💡 Explanation

Composite volcanoes, or stratovolcanoes, are built up by many layers of hardened lava, tephra, pumice, and ash.

In the context of tsunami warning systems, what is the primary purpose of a DART buoy?

💡 Explanation

Deep-ocean Assessment and Reporting of Tsunamis (DART) buoys measure sea-level height changes at the ocean floor to provide early warning of tsunamis.

The 'Shadow Zone' of an earthquake refers to regions where:

💡 Explanation

The shadow zone is an area on Earth's surface where seismographs cannot detect direct earthquake waves due to refraction at the Earth's core-mantle boundary.

What is the term for a superheated, fast-moving current of gas and volcanic matter that flows away from a volcano?

💡 Explanation

A pyroclastic flow is a highly destructive, fast-moving avalanche of hot gas, ash, and rock fragments from an erupting volcano.

Which layer of the Earth's interior is primarily responsible for the convection currents that drive tectonic plate movement?

💡 Explanation

Convection currents in the semi-solid mantle drive the motion of the rigid tectonic plates floating above it.

When a tsunami approaches the shoreline, what happens to the wave characteristics?

💡 Explanation

As a tsunami moves into shallower coastal water, friction slows the wave (decreasing velocity), causing the water to pile up and height to increase.

What does the 'Moment Magnitude Scale' (Mw) specifically account for that the Richter scale does not?

💡 Explanation

The Moment Magnitude Scale is a more accurate measure because it uses the physical properties of the fault, including the size of the rupture area and the amount of slip, to calculate total energy.
