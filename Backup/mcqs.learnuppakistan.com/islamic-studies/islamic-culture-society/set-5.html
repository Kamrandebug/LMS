Which month in the Islamic calendar is strictly prohibited for warfare, historically known as the 'Month of Peace'?

💡 Explanation

Rajab is one of the four sacred months in the Islamic calendar, traditionally observed as a period where fighting was forbidden.

What is the literal meaning of the Islamic month 'Muharram'?

💡 Explanation

The word Muharram is derived from the word 'haram', meaning forbidden or sacred, signifying that warfare is prohibited during this time.

The event of 'Isra and Mi'raj' is commemorated on which night of the Islamic calendar?

💡 Explanation

According to Islamic tradition, the Isra and Mi'raj (Night Journey and Ascension) occurred on the 27th night of the month of Rajab.

Which Islamic month follows the month of Dhu al-Hijjah?

💡 Explanation

The Islamic calendar begins with Muharram and ends with Dhu al-Hijjah; therefore, the cycle restarts with Muharram.

The 'Day of Ashura' falls on which date of the Islamic month of Muharram?

💡 Explanation

Ashura is observed on the 10th day of Muharram, marking significant historical events in Islamic history.

Which month is known as the month of 'Rabi al-Awwal' in terms of its literal seasonal origin?

💡 Explanation

Rabi al-Awwal translates to 'the first spring' or 'the first month of spring', referring to the season in which it was originally named.

What is the significance of the 15th night of the month of Sha'ban, often called 'Shab-e-Barat'?

💡 Explanation

Shab-e-Barat, falling on the 15th of Sha'ban, is widely regarded as a night of divine mercy, forgiveness, and the recording of destiny.

The month of Ramadan is specifically mentioned in the Quran for which primary reason?

💡 Explanation

Surah Al-Baqarah (2:185) states that the Quran was revealed in the month of Ramadan as guidance for mankind.

Which of the following months is not one of the 'Ashur-e-Hurum' (Four Sacred Months)?

💡 Explanation

The four sacred months are Muharram, Rajab, Dhu al-Qa'dah, and Dhu al-Hijjah.

The Islamic calendar is based on which cycle?

💡 Explanation

The Islamic Hijri calendar is a purely lunar calendar consisting of 12 months based on the moon's phases.

Which month in the Islamic calendar is traditionally recognized as the month of 'Safar' which literally means 'void' or 'empty'?

💡 Explanation

Safar is the second month of the Islamic calendar and its name historically refers to houses being empty when people went out to gather food.

Which night is widely regarded by scholars as the most probable night for 'Laylat al-Qadr' (The Night of Decree)?

💡 Explanation

While Laylat al-Qadr is sought in the last ten odd nights, the 27th night is most widely cited by tradition as the expected night.

The month of 'Rajab' is one of the four sacred months. What is the literal meaning of the word 'Rajab'?

💡 Explanation

The word Rajab is derived from 'tarjib', which means to respect, honor, or hold in high esteem.

Which month is historically referred to as 'Rabi al-Thani' in the Hijri calendar?

💡 Explanation

Rabi al-Thani is the fourth month of the Islamic calendar, following Rabi al-Awwal.

In the Islamic lunar calendar, what is the specific term for a year that contains 355 days instead of 354?

💡 Explanation

A Kabisah year is an intercalary year in the Islamic calendar that includes an extra day (355 days total) to keep the lunar phases aligned with the calendar.

Which of the following months is the last month of the Islamic calendar?

💡 Explanation

Dhu al-Hijjah is the twelfth and final month of the Islamic lunar calendar, during which the Hajj pilgrimage takes place.

What does the name 'Jumada al-Awwal' literally translate to in relation to its seasonal origins?

💡 Explanation

Jumada translates to 'dry' or 'arid' land, referring to the time when water would dry up due to the season.

Which month precedes Ramadan in the Islamic calendar?

💡 Explanation

Sha'ban is the eighth month of the Islamic calendar and immediately precedes the holy month of Ramadan.

The festival of 'Eid al-Fitr' marks the beginning of which month?

💡 Explanation

Eid al-Fitr is celebrated on the 1st of Shawwal, marking the end of the fasting month of Ramadan.

Which Islamic month is characterized by the 'Ayyam al-Bid' (the bright days) occurring in the middle?

💡 Explanation

Ayyam al-Bid refers to the 13th, 14th, and 15th of every Islamic month, when the moon is full and bright.
