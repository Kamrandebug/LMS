Which of the following is considered the primary source of Islamic law (Fiqh) according to the consensus of all Sunni schools of thought?

💡 Explanation

The Quran is the primary and fundamental source of Islamic jurisprudence (Fiqh) from which all other sources derive their authority.

Who is the founder of the Hanafi school of Fiqh?

💡 Explanation

Imam Abu Hanifa (Nu'man bin Thabit) is the founder of the Hanafi school, which is known for its emphasis on Qiyas (analogy) and Ra'y (reasoned opinion).

What does the term 'Ijma' refer to in Islamic jurisprudence?

💡 Explanation

Ijma represents the consensus of Muslim jurists on a legal matter after the death of the Prophet Muhammad (PBUH).

The Maliki school of Fiqh places particular emphasis on the practices of the people of which city?

💡 Explanation

Imam Malik gave special importance to 'Amal ahl al-Madinah' (the practice of the people of Madinah), considering it a strong evidence of the Sunnah.

Which school of jurisprudence is known for writing 'Al-Risala', the first systematic book on the principles of Islamic law (Usul al-Fiqh)?

💡 Explanation

Imam al-Shafi'i authored 'Al-Risala', which laid down the formal methodology for Usul al-Fiqh, integrating Quran, Sunnah, Ijma, and Qiyas.

Which term is used for 'Analogical Deduction' in Islamic legal theory?

💡 Explanation

Qiyas is the process by which the laws of the Quran and Sunnah are extended to new cases by comparing them to original cases based on a shared effective cause (illah).

Who is the eponymous founder of the Hanbali school of thought?

💡 Explanation

Imam Ahmad bin Hanbal established the Hanbali school, which is recognized for its strict adherence to Hadith and traditional texts.

What is the meaning of 'Ijtihad' in the context of Fiqh?

💡 Explanation

Ijtihad is the effort of a jurist to derive a ruling from the sources of law in cases where there is no explicit text from the Quran or Hadith.

The Jafari school of jurisprudence is primarily associated with which branch of Islam?

💡 Explanation

The Jafari school is the predominant school of jurisprudence for the Twelver Shia branch, named after Imam Jafar al-Sadiq.

Which source of Fiqh refers to 'Juridical Preference' or favoring a ruling that serves justice better?

💡 Explanation

Istihsan (juridical preference) allows a jurist to depart from a strict Qiyas in favor of a ruling that better aligns with the overall objectives of Shariah.

Which scholar is the author of 'Al-Muwatta', a foundational text of the Maliki school of jurisprudence?

💡 Explanation

Al-Muwatta is the earliest collection of Hadith and Fiqh compiled by Imam Malik bin Anas, which serves as a cornerstone for the Maliki school.

The principle of 'Maslaha Mursala' (Public Interest) is most prominently utilized as a source of law in which school of Fiqh?

💡 Explanation

The Maliki school is known for giving significant weight to Maslaha Mursala, which refers to rulings based on public welfare where there is no specific textual evidence.

In the hierarchy of Islamic legal sources, what does 'Qiyas' specifically involve?

💡 Explanation

Qiyas is the process of analogical deduction where the ruling of an original case (Asl) is applied to a new case (Far') due to a shared effective cause (Illah).

What is the primary distinction between 'Usul al-Fiqh' and 'Furu al-Fiqh'?

💡 Explanation

Usul al-Fiqh relates to the principles and methodology of derivation, while Furu al-Fiqh refers to the substantive laws (the branches) derived from these principles.

The term 'Fatwa' refers to what in Islamic jurisprudence?

💡 Explanation

A Fatwa is a formal legal ruling or interpretation given by a Mufti (legal expert) in response to a question or case.

Which of the following is considered a 'secondary' or 'supplementary' source of Islamic law?

💡 Explanation

While the Quran, Sunnah, and Ijma are primary/foundational, Urf (custom) is considered a supplementary source that may be valid if it does not contradict the primary texts.

What is the literal meaning of the word 'Fiqh'?

💡 Explanation

Linguistically, Fiqh means to understand or comprehend deeply; in technical terms, it refers to the science of deriving Islamic laws from their sources.

Which category of action in Fiqh is defined as 'Mandatory' where failure to perform it results in sin?

💡 Explanation

Fard (or Wajib) denotes acts that are strictly obligatory, and their omission is considered a transgression in Islamic law.

The school of thought that emphasizes the 'Zahiri' (literal) interpretation of texts is primarily associated with which scholar?

💡 Explanation

The Zahiri school, founded by Dawud al-Zahiri, argues for the literal meaning of the Quran and Hadith and rejects analogy (Qiyas) if it deviates from the text.

What is 'Sadd al-Dhara'i' in Islamic legal theory?

💡 Explanation

Sadd al-Dhara'i is a legal principle employed to prohibit acts that are outwardly permissible but lead to forbidden or harmful consequences.
